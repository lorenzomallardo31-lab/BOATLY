# BOATLY — BRAND IDENTITY

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Phase B — Brand Identity Approved Specification
**Brand:** Boatly
**Initial market:** Italy
**Product:** Boat Rental Marketplace + Operator Management SaaS

---

# 1. BRAND PURPOSE

Boatly exists to make recreational boat rental easier, more transparent and more manageable for both sides of the market.

For customers:

finding and booking the right boat should feel as simple as booking other modern travel experiences.

For rental operators:

running a fleet should not require fragmented calendars, WhatsApp conversations, spreadsheets and disconnected tools.

Boatly connects:

DISCOVERY

*

BOOKING

*

OPERATIONS.

---

# 2. BRAND ESSENCE

Boatly represents:

**Freedom, made simple.**

The emotional promise is freedom on the water.

The functional promise is simplicity and control.

Boatly should never feel bureaucratic even when the underlying platform handles complex:

* booking rules;
* payments;
* compliance;
* availability;
* fleet management.

Complexity remains behind the interface.

---

# 3. BRAND POSITIONING

Boatly is a modern boat-rental platform that combines:

a customer marketplace

with

a professional management system for rental operators.

Positioning:

> The simplest way to discover, book and manage boat rentals.

Boatly should occupy the space between:

* traditional fragmented local rental businesses;
* generic tourism booking sites;
* existing boat marketplaces;
* complex fleet-management software.

The differentiation is:

**marketplace + operational software in one ecosystem.**

---

# 4. BRAND PROMISE

For customers:

> Find the right boat, know what you're paying, and book with confidence.

For operators:

> Manage your entire rental business from one place.

Overall:

> Boatly makes getting on the water simpler.

---

# 5. PRIMARY BRAND IDEA

The central concept behind Boatly is:

**FROM SHORE TO SEA, WITHOUT FRICTION.**

Customer journey:

Idea

↓

Search

↓

Boat

↓

Booking

↓

Water.

Operator journey:

Fleet

↓

Availability

↓

Booking

↓

Operation

↓

Revenue.

Boatly removes friction between each step.

---

# 6. BRAND PILLARS

## 6.1 SIMPLICITY

Boat rental can contain:

* licence rules;
* deposits;
* skipper options;
* schedules;
* extras;
* cancellations;
* payments.

The interface should still feel simple.

Brand implication:

clear language, clear hierarchy, minimal unnecessary UI.

---

## 6.2 TRUST

Customers need confidence before spending hundreds or thousands of euros.

Operators need confidence before putting their business and customer calendar into Boatly.

Trust is communicated through:

* transparent pricing;
* real availability;
* professional imagery;
* verified information;
* clear policies;
* clean interface;
* predictable interactions.

---

## 6.3 FREEDOM

The emotional reason people rent boats is not software.

It is:

* sea;
* discovery;
* summer;
* privacy;
* adventure;
* relaxation;
* experiences with friends and family.

Boatly must keep that emotional dimension visible.

---

## 6.4 CONTROL

For operators, Boatly means operational control.

Examples:

* one calendar;
* one fleet view;
* one customer history;
* one availability source;
* one operational workspace.

The operator product should feel capable and professional without becoming visually heavy.

---

# 7. BRAND PERSONALITY

Boatly is:

* modern;
* clear;
* trustworthy;
* Mediterranean;
* energetic;
* competent;
* approachable;
* premium enough to inspire confidence;
* accessible enough for normal customers.

Boatly is NOT:

* elitist;
* nautical-club traditional;
* overly luxurious;
* childish;
* overly playful;
* corporate-grey;
* aggressive;
* crypto/startup-looking;
* visually noisy.

---

# 8. BRAND CHARACTER

If Boatly were a person, it would be:

A knowledgeable local friend who knows boating well, uses modern technology, explains things simply and helps you get on the water without unnecessary complications.

It would not speak like:

a maritime lawyer.

It would not speak like:

a luxury yacht broker.

It would not speak like:

a teenage travel influencer.

---

# 9. TARGET CUSTOMER

Primary B2C audience:

* tourists;
* couples;
* groups of friends;
* families;
* people planning coastal holidays;
* customers interested in day rentals;
* customers comparing local operators;
* Italian and international visitors.

Customer technical expertise may vary greatly.

Some customers have never rented a boat before.

Therefore Boatly must never assume nautical expertise.

---

# 10. TARGET OPERATOR

Primary B2B audience:

professional rental companies with approximately:

3–30 boats initially.

Typical operator pain points:

* bookings through WhatsApp;
* phone reservations;
* paper calendars;
* spreadsheets;
* multiple employees;
* changing prices;
* customer information scattered across systems;
* double-booking risk;
* no centralized fleet view;
* marketplace commissions;
* manual availability updates.

The brand must feel professional enough for these businesses to trust Boatly with daily operations.

---

# 11. MASTERBRAND STRATEGY

Use one master brand:

**Boatly**

Do not create separate unrelated brands for:

* marketplace;
* operator dashboard;
* admin.

Product areas can be referred to descriptively.

Examples:

**Boatly**

Customer marketplace.

**Boatly for Operators**

Commercial/operator-facing communication.

**Boatly Admin**

Internal product terminology only.

The customer should not feel they are moving between different companies.

---

**Boatly**

BOATLY

Not:

Boatly App

unless context explicitly requires "app".

# 13. NAME CHARACTERISTICS

The name Boatly works because it is:

* short;
* international;
* directly related to boating;
* easy to pronounce;
* digital-product friendly;
* suitable for B2C and B2B;
* flexible enough for international expansion.

The visual identity should make the name feel like a technology/travel brand rather than a traditional nautical company.

# 14. PRONUNCIATION

Preferred pronunciation:

**boat-ly**

The name should remain understandable internationally.

No attempt should be made to Italianize the written form.

# 18. BRAND VOICE

Boatly speaks in a way that is:

* direct;
* clear;
* concise;
* human;
* helpful;
* confident;
* professional.

Avoid unnecessary technical jargon.

Avoid exaggerated advertising language.

---

# 19. CUSTOMER TONE

Customer-facing tone:

friendly + simple + reassuring.

Example:

Good:

> Scegli quando vuoi partire.

Bad:

> Configura l'intervallo temporale della prenotazione.

Good:

> Per questa barca è richiesta la patente nautica.

Bad:

> L'unità è soggetta ai requisiti abilitativi previsti dalla normativa applicabile.

Legal detail can remain available in explanatory content, but primary UX copy stays understandable.

---

# 20. OPERATOR TONE

Operator-facing tone:

professional + efficient + operational.

Example:

Good:

> Il gommone è già occupato dalle 10:00 alle 14:00.

Bad:

> Impossibile completare l'operazione a causa di un conflitto temporale della risorsa.

Good:

> Mancano 12 giorni alla scadenza dell'assicurazione.

Bad:

> Documento prossimo allo stato EXPIRED.

---

# 21. ADMIN TONE

Admin UI should be:

precise

*

neutral

*

unambiguous.

Examples:

> Documento rifiutato

> Rimborso in elaborazione

> Noleggiatore sospeso

Avoid playful language in:

* payments;
* refunds;
* compliance;
* moderation;
* account suspension.

---

# 22. MICROCOPY PRINCIPLE

Prefer:

verb + object.

Examples:

* Cerca barche
* Prenota ora
* Aggiungi barca
* Crea prenotazione
* Blocca calendario
* Invita collaboratore
* Carica documento
* Assegna skipper
* Emetti rimborso

Avoid vague CTAs such as:

* Continua
* Vai
* Procedi

when the next action has a meaningful consequence.

---

# 23. PAYMENT CTA

Final payment action must be explicit.

Conceptual examples:

> Prenota e paga

or legally approved equivalent.

Never hide the payment obligation behind generic:

> Continua

---

# 24. ERROR VOICE

Errors should tell the user:

1. what happened;
2. what they can do next.

Example:

> Questa barca non è più disponibile nell'orario selezionato. Prova un altro orario o torna ai risultati.

Not:

> Booking conflict error.

---

# 25. SUCCESS VOICE

Success should be calm rather than excessively celebratory.

Example:

> Prenotazione confermata

Supporting:

> Trovi tutti i dettagli e il contratto nella tua area personale.

Avoid:

> INCREDIBILE! SEI PRONTO PER L'AVVENTURA!!! 🎉🚤🌊

---

# 26. BRAND LANGUAGE

Initial product language:

Italian.

Architecture must remain compatible with:

* English;
* other future languages.

Do not embed untranslated Italian text deeply into business logic.

Future localization should be possible.

* captain's wheel;

* compass rose;

# 56. COPY RULE — TRANSPARENCY

Never use misleading urgency.

Avoid:

> SOLO 1 BARCA RIMASTA!!!

unless the statement is actually derived from relevant real inventory.

Avoid fake countdown timers.

Avoid fake scarcity.

---

# 57. COPY RULE — VERIFICATION

Only use words like:

* verified;
* certified;
* approved;
* secure;

when Boatly has a defined process supporting that claim.

---

# 58. COPY RULE — PRICE

Price copy should clearly distinguish:

* starting price;
* calculated price;
* extras;
* deposit;
* mandatory fees;
* total.

Avoid hidden mandatory charges revealed only at final checkout.

---

# 59. COPY RULE — NAUTICAL COMPLEXITY

Customer language should simplify without becoming legally inaccurate.

Example:

Primary:

> Patente nautica richiesta

> I requisiti dipendono dalla configurazione della barca e dal tipo di navigazione.

Legal detail can live behind:

> Scopri di più.
